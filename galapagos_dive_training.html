Before the first rep

The honest part about currents

Strength matters, but in Galápagos the goal is rarely to out-swim a current. At Darwin and Wolf the standard play is to drop fast, get low, tuck behind structure or use a reef hook, and let the current pass over you while you watch the hammerheads. The diver who burns through a tank fighting the flow is making a technique error, not just a fitness one.

So this program trains two things at once

1. The fitness: stronger legs/hips so finning costs you less effort, and a bigger aerobic engine so you don't go into oxygen debt during the exertion spikes that do happen — negative entries, swimming into a hook position, crossing a channel.

2. The breathing. Losing your breath in current stretches is largely CO₂ build-up from over-exertion plus shallow rushed breathing. The aerobic + breath-control work below targets exactly that. On the boat, also focus on slow nasal-style exhales, good trim, and efficient flutter/frog kicks — ask your divemaster about reef-hook technique for Darwin/Wolf.

How to read the cards: every exercise is tagged BOTH (works either way), WEIGHTS, or TRAVEL (no equipment). Each has plain-language form cues, a rep prescription, a video link, and — where useful — a travel swap for when the dumbbells aren't with you.

Goal 1 · Holding position

Legs & hips

Quads, glutes, hamstrings and calves are your finning engine. We build strength first, then add the single-leg and explosive work that translates to controlling your body in moving water.

Goblet Squat

Weights

The foundation. Holding the weight at your chest keeps you upright and is gentle on the lower back — ideal starting from sedentary.

Cues:

  • Hold one dumbbell vertically against your chest, both hands cupping the top end.
  • Feet a bit wider than shoulders, toes turned out slightly.
  • Sit down and back like lowering into a chair; knees track over toes, don't cave inward.
  • Go until thighs are about parallel to the floor, chest tall. Drive up through your heels.
3 sets × 10–12 reps
Travel swap → Bodyweight squat, or slow tempo (3 seconds down) to make it harder without weight.
Watch: Goblet Squat form

Reverse Lunge

Both

Single-leg strength and balance — the closest dry-land match to stabilizing yourself in current. Stepping backward is knee-friendly.

Cues:

  • Stand tall, optionally a dumbbell in each hand at your sides.
  • Step one foot straight back, lower until both knees bend ~90°, back knee hovers above the floor.
  • Keep your torso upright; push through the front heel to stand back up.
  • Alternate legs, or do all reps on one side then switch.
3 sets × 8–10 per leg
Travel swap → Same movement, no weights. Add a small hop at the top for a challenge once strong.
Watch: Reverse Lunge form

Romanian Deadlift (RDL)

Weights

Targets the hamstrings and glutes — the back-of-leg power for your kick. Uses the bar.

Cues:

  • Hold the bar (or dumbbells) in front of your thighs, knees softly bent.
  • Push your hips backward — like closing a door with your butt — letting the bar slide down your legs.
  • Keep your back flat, never rounded. Feel the stretch in your hamstrings; stop around mid-shin.
  • Squeeze your glutes to stand back up. Slow and controlled.
3 sets × 8–10 reps
Travel swap → Single-leg RDL with no weight — balance on one leg, hinge forward reaching the other leg straight back. Brutal for balance, perfect for diving.
Watch: Romanian Deadlift form

Bulgarian Split Squat

Both

The single best single-leg strength builder. Tough — introduce it around week 4.

Cues:

  • Stand a couple feet in front of a chair/couch; rest the top of one foot on it behind you.
  • Lower straight down on the front leg until the thigh is about parallel.
  • Front knee tracks over the foot; keep your weight in the front heel.
  • Hold dumbbells at your sides to add load.
3 sets × 8 per leg
Travel swap → Bodyweight only — a hotel chair works fine.
Watch: Bulgarian Split Squat

Calf Raises

Both

Your calves do enormous work finning. Easy to do anywhere, anytime.

Cues:

  • Stand with the balls of your feet on the edge of a step, heels hanging off.
  • Rise as high as possible onto your toes, pause, lower slowly below the step level for a stretch.
  • Hold a dumbbell in one hand (other on a wall for balance) to load it.
3 sets × 15–20 reps
Travel swap → Identical with bodyweight; do single-leg to increase difficulty.
Watch: Calf Raise form
Goal 2 · Not running out of air

Breath & engine

This is the piece that directly fixes "losing my breath in current stretches." You're building aerobic capacity so exertion costs you less oxygen, plus training calm, deep breathing patterns. Do at least two of these sessions a week.

The single most transferable workout: Zone 2 cardio

Steady effort where you can still hold a conversation but wouldn't want to sing — about 30–45 minutes. Brisk walking on an incline, cycling, swimming, rowing, or jogging all count. This raises the aerobic base that keeps you from gassing out. If you do nothing else for fitness, do this 2–3× a week.

Lap Swimming (best option)

Travel

Nothing matches the specificity of swimming for dive fitness — same breathing rhythm, same muscles, same horizontal position.

How:

  • Freestyle or breaststroke, steady pace, breathing on a rhythm (e.g. every 3 strokes).
  • Build from 20 to 40 minutes continuous over the weeks.
  • Add interval sets later: swim hard one length, easy the next — this mimics current exertion spikes.
2× per week, 20–40 min Watch: Freestyle breathing technique

Interval Cardio (HIIT-lite)

Travel

Trains your body to recover fast between bursts of effort — exactly what current diving demands.

Simple version:

  • 30 seconds hard effort (fast jog, bike sprint, or burpees), then 90 seconds easy.
  • Repeat 6–8 rounds. Total ~15 minutes plus warm-up.
  • Focus on bringing your breathing back under control during the easy phase — that's the skill.
1× per week Watch: Beginner HIIT structure

Box Breathing / Breath Control

Travel

A daily 5-minute drill to train slow, deep, controlled breathing — the calm you want underwater.

How:

  • Inhale slowly through the nose for 4 counts.
  • Hold for 4 counts.
  • Exhale slowly for 4 counts (longer is even better — try 6).
  • Hold empty for 4 counts. Repeat for 5 minutes.
Daily, 5 min
Why it helps → A long, relaxed exhale is what flushes CO₂ and stops the panicky "can't catch my breath" feeling. Practicing dry makes it automatic wet. Never practice breath-holds in or under water alone.

Goal 3 · The dive bag

Arms & grip

Lifting heavy luggage and tanks is about pulling strength, shoulder stability, and grip — not biceps curls. Here's what actually carries the bag.

Bent-Over Row

Weights

The number-one luggage-lifting exercise. Builds the whole back and arms in the exact pulling pattern of hauling a bag off a carousel.

Cues:

  • Hinge forward at the hips (back flat), bar or dumbbells hanging at arm's length.
  • Pull the weight to your lower ribs, squeezing your shoulder blades together.
  • Lower under control. Don't yank with your lower back.
3 sets × 10 reps
Travel swap → Inverted rows — lie under a sturdy table and pull your chest to the edge. Or use a backpack full of books.
Watch: Bent-Over Row form

Overhead Press

Weights

For hoisting bags into overhead bins. Builds shoulder strength and stability.

Cues:

  • Dumbbells or bar at shoulder height, feet shoulder-width, core braced.
  • Press straight overhead until arms lock out; keep ribs down, don't arch your back.
  • Lower under control to the shoulders.
3 sets × 8–10 reps
Travel swap → Pike push-ups — hips piked high, lower your head toward the floor between your hands.
Watch: Overhead Press form

Push-Ups

Travel

The universal upper-body builder — chest, shoulders, triceps, core. Scalable from knees to full.

Cues:

  • Hands slightly wider than shoulders, body in one straight line head to heels.
  • Lower until your chest is just above the floor, elbows at roughly 45° to your body.
  • Push back up. Too hard? Drop to knees or push from an elevated surface (counter/wall).
3 sets × as many as good form allows Watch: Push-Up form

Farmer's Carry

Both

The most literal luggage-training exercise: pick up heavy things and walk. Builds crushing grip strength and a stable core.

Cues:

  • Hold a heavy dumbbell in each hand at your sides.
  • Stand tall, shoulders back, and walk 30–40 steps with control.
  • Grip hard the whole way. Rest, repeat.
3 rounds × 30–40 steps
Travel swap → Two heavy backpacks, water jugs, or — fittingly — your packed dive bags.
Watch: Farmer's Carry
The quiet hero

Core & trim

A strong core is what lets you hold horizontal trim and stay stable when current pushes you around. All bodyweight — do these anywhere, twice a week.

Plank

Travel

Cues: Forearms and toes on the floor, body in one straight line, squeeze glutes and abs. Don't let hips sag or pike. Breathe normally.

3 sets × 30–60 sec hold Watch: Plank form

Bird Dog

Travel

Cues: On hands and knees, extend opposite arm and leg straight out, hold briefly, return. Keep hips level — no twisting. This directly trains the anti-rotation stability you use to hold trim.

3 sets × 8 per side Watch: Bird Dog form

Superman Hold

Travel

Cues: Lie face down, lift arms, chest and legs off the floor, hold. Strengthens the lower back that keeps you arched and streamlined while finning.

3 sets × 20–30 sec Watch: Superman form

A simple split that hits everything without overlap. Adjust days to your life — the order matters more than which calendar day.

Travel weeks

When you're away from the dumbbells, swap to the TRAVEL versions on every card and keep the same 4-day rhythm. Squats, lunges, push-ups, rows under a table, planks, carries with your bags, and a swim or jog wherever you are will hold all your progress. Box breathing every day regardless.

Don't skip this

The 12-week ramp

Starting sedentary, the biggest risk is doing too much too soon and getting hurt before September. Progress in phases — earn each jump.

1–3

Groove the patterns

Bodyweight or light weights only. Learn every movement. 2 sets, easy reps. Build the habit and start Zone 2 cardio. Expect soreness; that's normal.

4–7

Build strength

Add weight to the dumbbell/bar lifts, move to 3 sets. Introduce Bulgarian split squats. Push swim/cardio toward 35–40 min. This is where strength really climbs.

8–10

Add power & intervals

Heavier loads, lower reps on main lifts. Add interval cardio and hard/easy swim sets to train recovery between exertion spikes — the current-specific adaptation.

11–12

Sharpen & taper

Maintain, don't max out. Keep movement quality high, prioritize swimming and breath work. Arrive in Galápagos strong and fresh, not beaten up.

Two rules that matter most

1. Consistency beats intensity. Four steady weeks of moderate work crush one brutal week followed by quitting. 2. Progress slowly. Add a little weight or a rep only when the current level feels manageable with good form. Sharp pain = stop; muscle soreness = fine.
